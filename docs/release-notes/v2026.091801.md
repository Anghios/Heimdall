# The passphrase generator speaks Spanish

Yesterday's release gave Heimdall a Spanish interface and said, in as many
words, that the passphrase generator was not part of it. It is now.

## A Spanish word list

The generator has a Spanish word list of 725 words, and it opens on that list
when Heimdall is in Spanish. Until now a Spanish profile opened on the English
one, and the language box offered nothing else to pick.

The words were chosen to need no accent and no n tilde, rather than taken
accented and stripped of it. The two are not the same thing: an accented Spanish
word with its accent removed is not a simpler spelling, it is a wrong one. The
French list has always worked the same way, for the same reason. A passphrase is
read off one screen and typed on whatever keyboard happens to be in front of
you.

The Spanish list is larger than the English and French ones, so a Spanish
passphrase of the same length is worth a little more. The strength figure the
tool shows is computed from the list actually in use, so it has been saying so
since the moment the list arrived.

## Picking a language actually picks it

Three separate places decided which word list a passphrase came from, and they
agreed only as long as there were two languages. The generator itself asked one
question: is the second entry selected? If yes, French. If no, English. Anything
beyond the second entry was English.

So a third language could be listed, chosen, and shown as chosen, while every
passphrase it produced was English. Nothing failed, because nothing compared the
three places to each other. They now read one table, and choosing a language
selects its words.

Saved presets keep the language they were saved with. The table that replaces
the three is deliberately append-only, because a preset stores the position of
the language rather than its name.

## The lists say what they hold

The English and French lists repeated a few words between them, eleven and
seven. Nothing was ever weaker than the strength shown, because the duplicates
were discarded as the file was read, but the files claimed to offer more than
they did. Both are cleaned up, and a repeated word is now an error rather than
something quietly dropped.
